## Where the funnel actually leaks

**113 impressions is the problem.** Not conversion.

Roughly a quarter of impressions become page views and a fifth of those become downloads — for an
app with 2 ratings and no reviews to speak of, neither of those is broken. The listing converts
acceptably for the tiny number of people who see it.

Almost nobody sees it. 113 impressions across the window is the entire ceiling on everything
downstream, and impressions are precisely what keywords and subtitle control. This is direct
evidence for the reposition: the bet is not "convert better", it is "be findable at all".

It also means the downstream numbers stay untrustworthy for a while. At 5 downloads, conversion
rate swings several points on a single install.

## How to read the after

**Watch impressions first.** That is the stage the reposition acts on, and the stage that currently
caps everything else. **113** is the number to beat.

**Judge absolute counts, not percentages.** Going from 1 page view a day to 3 is a 200% lift and
means nothing. The question the reposition answers is binary at this stage:

> Does sustained, non-zero **App Store Search** traffic appear where there was none?

That is the signal. Search traffic is what keywords move; Browse and Referrer are not.

**Do not read conversion rate as a result.** At 5 downloads it moves several points per install.
It becomes meaningful only once impressions are an order of magnitude higher — and if impressions
climb while conversion falls, that is expected, not failure: a wider net catches more people who
were never going to install.

**Secondary, in rough order of usefulness:**

1. Search impressions existing at all — currently near zero, so any consistent daily figure is new
   information.
2. Product page views → downloads conversion rate. With traffic this thin the rate is noise now,
   but it becomes the real measure once volume arrives.
3. Campaign rows appearing under Acquisition → Campaigns. Needs five installs through a shared link
   before Apple will render it, so expect nothing here for a while.

**Give it 30 days minimum.** Apple re-indexes keywords over days, not hours, and at this volume a
week of data is indistinguishable from chance.
